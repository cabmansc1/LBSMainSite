# Proof and revision terms

Written once, reused in every print quote. The point of them is not legal
protection — it is that the awkward conversations happen before the job rather
than after it.

Two versions below. Use the **short version** in the body of a quote email. Keep
the **full version** for anything over a few hundred dollars, or the first time
you work with someone.

---

## Short version — paste into a quote

> **Before we print:** I'll send a PDF proof. Print is final once it goes to
> press, so please check spelling, phone numbers, addresses and offer details
> carefully — those are the ones that hurt.
>
> **Revisions:** one round of changes is included. Further rounds are $45 each.
>
> **Timing:** turnaround starts when you approve the proof, not when you order.
> Standard is [X] business days plus delivery.
>
> **If something goes wrong:** if the printer makes an error, they reprint it at
> no cost to you. If an error makes it through onto an approved proof, a reprint
> is chargeable — which is why the proof stage matters.
>
> **Colour:** screens and paper don't match exactly. Colours will be close but
> not identical to what you see on your monitor.

## Full version

### 1. Artwork

If I designed your postcard ad or other artwork, I already have everything I
need and there is no charge to adapt it for this product.

If you're supplying your own artwork, I need print-ready files — 300 DPI, CMYK,
with 1/8" bleed. If what you have doesn't meet that, I can rebuild it, and I'll
quote that as design time before starting rather than surprising you with it
afterwards.

### 2. Proofs

Every job gets a PDF proof before it goes to press. Nothing is printed until you
reply to approve it.

**Please check the proof properly.** The things that go wrong most often are the
ones that are easiest to skim past: phone numbers, email addresses, website
addresses, prices, dates, and the spelling of names. A printed error is a
reprint, not an edit.

Approval by email is enough — "approved" in a reply is fine.

### 3. Revisions

One round of revisions is included in the quoted price. A round means all your
changes sent together, not one change at a time.

Additional rounds are $45 each. I'll always tell you when a request crosses into
a chargeable round before doing the work, never after.

Changes to the *content* of a job after design has started — new copy, a
different offer, a change of size — are a new quote rather than a revision.

### 4. Turnaround

Turnaround begins when you approve the proof, not when you place the order.
Standard production is [X] business days, plus delivery time.

If you need it faster, say so up front. Rush handling is available at a [X]%
uplift on the whole job, subject to the printer's schedule — it is not always
possible, and I would rather tell you that on day one.

**If you have a hard deadline — an event, an opening, a mailing date — tell me
before we start.** I will build the schedule backwards from it.

### 5. Errors and reprints

- **Printer error** — wrong stock, wrong size, poor quality, damaged in
  production: reprinted at no cost to you.
- **My error** — something that was correct on your instructions but wrong on
  the proof I sent: reprinted at no cost to you.
- **Error on an approved proof** — a typo, a wrong number, an outdated address
  that made it through approval: a reprint is chargeable at cost. I don't make
  margin on a reprint in this situation.

That third one is the reason the proof stage exists, and the reason I would
rather you took an extra day over it than approved it the same afternoon.

### 6. Colour

Screens emit light; paper reflects it. They will never match exactly. Printed
colour will be close to your proof but not identical, and this is normal rather
than a fault.

If exact colour matching matters — matching an existing sign, a vehicle wrap, or
a logo standard — say so before we print. A physical proof can usually be
arranged at additional cost.

### 7. Quantities

Print pricing is tiered, and the tiers are steep. Ordering 500 twice costs
noticeably more than ordering 1,000 once. If your quantity is close to the next
tier, I'll tell you what the next tier costs so you can decide.

Print runs can vary slightly from the ordered quantity — a small over or under
is standard in the trade and is not a shortfall.

### 8. Delivery

Orders ship to the address you give me. Please check it before approving.

Boxes occasionally arrive late or damaged. If that happens, tell me straight
away and I'll deal with the printer and the carrier — that is my job, not
yours. Keep the packaging until it is resolved.

### 9. Payment

Payment is due on approval of the proof, before the job goes to press.

Existing advertisers with an account can have print added to their next invoice
where it falls below the minimum order value — it is usually less hassle for
both of us than running a separate transaction.

### 10. Cancellation

Cancel any time before you approve the proof at no cost, other than design time
already spent if design was quoted separately.

Once a job is at press it cannot be cancelled — it is being manufactured to
order and there is nothing to restock.

## Notes for me, not the customer

- `[X]` placeholders need filling from the printer's actual turnaround and the
  rush uplift on the Setup tab of the rate sheet.
- The $45 extra revision figure comes from the Design Fees tab. If it changes
  there, change it here.
- Section 5's third case is the one that gets tested. Having it written down and
  sent in advance is the whole reason it stays a conversation rather than an
  argument.
- Don't send the full version for a $60 reorder of business cards. The short
  version in the quote is proportionate for routine work.
